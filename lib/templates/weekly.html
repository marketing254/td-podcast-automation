Most practice growth plans start strong in January.


But by mid-year, many of them are forgotten. The goals were discussed. The whiteboard was filled. Then daily work took over.


This week on The Thriving Dentist Show, Gary and Naren share a simple mid-year reset to help practices refocus before the year slips away.


There are still five and a half months left in 2026. That is enough time to make real progress if the team gets clear on the right priorities.


Gary explains why every practice should focus on no more than four key priorities for the rest of the year.


Each one should be clear, measurable, and owned by one person.


Instead of saying “grow Invisalign,” set a real target. Instead of hoping for more reviews, track the number each month. Instead of checking progress once in a while, review it in every team meeting.


The goal is simple: do not set it and forget it. Check the plan, adjust it, and keep moving.

One of the best stories from the episode is about a practice that was struggling to get Google reviews.


In January, they had 1 review.

In February, they had 3.

In March, they had 2.


Gary had suggested a small team incentive, but the doctor did not want to do it. He felt that getting reviews should simply be part of the team’s job.


Then the practice tried it.


The offer was simple: if the team reached 15 or more reviews in a month, each team member would receive $100.


The result:

• April: 24 reviews
• May: 27 reviews


The doctor later told Gary, “I was wrong.”


It is a simple example, but an important one. When the team has a clear goal, a clear owner, and a clear reason to care, results can change fast.


Naren also breaks down what it takes to create consistent new patient growth.



The benchmarks are specific:


• 100 or more keyword rankings on page one
• 20 or more new patient calls per month
• 10 or more paragraph-length Google reviews per month
• Appointments available within two to three days
• Phone conversion at 70% to 80%


If one of those numbers is weak, the practice may still be spending money on marketing, but not getting the results it should.


Naren also shares an AI search tip that is worth hearing.


Practices with 100 or more keyword rankings are seeing much stronger visibility in Google AI Overview. Practices below that level are often not being cited at all.


That matters because patients are no longer only searching on Google. They are also using AI tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overview to ask which dentist they should choose.
