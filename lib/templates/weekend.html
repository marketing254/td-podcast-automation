---- 3 Takeaways Worth Your Weekend Listen ----

1. Choose fewer goals

One reason growth plans fall apart is that the team is trying to focus on too many things at once.


Gary recommends choosing no more than four major goals for the year. These should be clear, measurable, and tied to real practice growth.


For example, your focus areas could include hygiene occupancy, new patient growth, Google reviews, or a high-value service like Invisalign or implants.


The key is focus. When the team knows exactly what matters, it becomes easier to track progress and take action.

2. Review the plan often

A growth plan should not be something you create in January and forget by February.


Gary’s advice is simple: assess and adjust.


Use short morning huddles to set the tone for the day. Use team meetings to review what is working, what is not working, and what needs to change.


This keeps the plan alive. It also helps the team stay connected to the goals instead of letting them fade into the background.

3. Use smart incentives

Not everything needs an incentive. Some things are simply part of the job.


But for the right goal, a smart incentive can create real movement.


Gary shared the story of one practice that was getting only a few Google reviews each month. After adding a simple team incentive, they jumped to more than 20 reviews in a month.


The lesson is not just about money. It is about giving the team a clear goal, a reason to care, and a win they can share together.
